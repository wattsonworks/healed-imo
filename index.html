What eight years actually looked like

Before: a body turned into a prison

I'm writing the ugly version on purpose, because every sanitized "gut health journey" online skips it, and skipping it is how people in this hole feel insane and alone. If any of this sounds like you — you are not imagining it, and you are not weak.

It started with the body breaking long before the gut did. In the army my kneecap shattered; I said for weeks I couldn't walk, and the doctor decided I was faking until I'd nearly lost the knee and needed emergency surgery. Then the second knee. Years later an ER doctor found that my tailbone had broken and healed crooked — six years of pelvic pain nobody had looked for. Sitting became torture. Every 20 seconds in a chair could end in a week with no stool and no gas at all.

Then came the eight years that nearly finished me:

  • Eight years of constipation that never ended, and a constant false alarm — the urgent feeling I had to go right now, while nothing came out. Not stool. Not even gas. The trapped gas was the worst part.
  • "Food doesn't go in and food doesn't come out." A doctor warned that soon they'd be emptying my bowel through an external bag for the rest of my life.
  • My lower belly would inflate like a balloon until my whole body locked — I couldn't bend to put on socks or underwear, couldn't turn over in bed, couldn't take a deep breath because the swollen bowel left no room for my lungs.
  • Blood didn't reach my legs — not sometimes, every morning, every time I sat or lay down. A burning under my navel I couldn't explain. Movements under my ribs like rats running inside me.
  • I was 45 kg, eating maybe once every two weeks. My floor was littered with used glycerin suppositories — I went through more than twenty in a day just to force something out, and they'd slip out of me while I walked.
  • When something finally came, it came without warning — sometimes in the middle of the street, on my own legs, in front of people. The shame of that hollows you out. I started to fear leaving the house at all. Whole nights without sleep from pain. And there were mornings I didn't want to wake up.

And the whole time, the system told me I was fine. Doctors discharged me again and again, said I was imagining it, said I was wasting their time. I matched diets to every disease I could find — IBS, Crohn's, colitis, autoimmune — and tried rifaximin, metronidazole, herbal and pharmaceutical antibiotics, steroids, peptides, pellets, tens of thousands of shekels of therapists and rehab. Nothing broke it. A lot of it broke me further.

So I started running experiments on my own body, because no one was coming to save me. That is where the protocol below came from.

The moment it started turning

The turn: a name, and spider webs

After years of dead ends I finally found the name: SIBO, and in my case IMO — methane-producing archaea overgrowing the gut and paralyzing it. That single word reorganized everything. I stopped chasing twenty diseases and started fighting one.

I went fully carnivore as a genuine last resort — after everything, and I mean everything, had already failed. Not "mostly meat," only meat, eventually 140+ days of it — because every carbohydrate felt like it was feeding something inside me and blowing me up from within. I started reading about intestinal biofilms, the slime fortress bacteria and archaea build around themselves, and added biofilm-disruption and mechanical clearance.

Then one day I started passing material that looked like spider webs — stringy, viscous, white-transparent sheets. That was the biofilm itself, the thing no test had ever found while I fell apart for years. It wasn't a magic cure. But it was the first hard proof that there was something real inside me that shouldn't be there — and that I was finally pulling it out.

Around then the other signs came too: my lifelong hay fever almost vanished, and a fungal nail started lifting off. The body was changing from the inside. That's when I knew I'd found the thread, and I started pulling.

The thing I most wish someone had told me on day one: this is a motility disease wearing a digestion costume. Kill everything you want — if the gut doesn't sweep itself clean between meals, it all grows back.
Know what you're fighting

The enemy: methane, biofilms, and a broken sweeper

You cannot out-supplement something you don't understand. Four mechanisms ran my whole illness, and every part of the protocol maps back to one of them.

1. Methane archaea paralyze the gut

Methanogens (an ancient kingdom of life — Methanobrevibacter smithii, not even bacteria) eat hydrogen gas and CO₂ and turn it into methane (CH₄). Methane physically slows the bowel by acting on its serotonin signalling — this is the core of Dr. Mark Pimentel's work. The result is a gas trap: pressurized, paralyzed bloat with no relief, because the gas can't move down to where you'd pass it.

2. The MMC — your housekeeping wave — stops running

Between meals, a healthy gut runs the Migrating Motor Complex: a wave that sweeps leftover food and bacteria out of the small intestine. It only runs fasted. Methane suppresses it. No sweep → bacteria sit and multiply → more methane → less sweep. Restoring the MMC (fasting windows + a prokinetic) is what makes a cure stick; without it every kill phase just buys a few weeks.

3. Biofilms are the fortress

The overgrowth hides inside biofilms — the "spider webs." Antimicrobials bounce off the shield, and it reseals in 24–48 hours, so it has to be broken daily for anything else to land.

4. It's a two-front war: upper GI vs lower GI

This was the model that finally made my protocol click. The hydrogen-producers and archaea live in different places and answer to different weapons:

Upper GI · hydrogen

Foot soldiers

Hydrogen-producing bacteria in the stomach/small intestine ferment carbs into H₂ — felt as burping and bloating. Cleared first, with cinnamon + black seed blended in water (liquid coats the whole tract; capsules pass as a bolus). When the burping stops completely, the upper front is won.

Lower GI · methane & sulfur

The generals

The archaea (methane) and sulfate-reducers (H₂S) sit lower down. They don't eat food — they eat the hydrogen the foot soldiers make. Starve the hydrogen and they go dormant; finish them with berberine + allicin (the gold-standard herbal pair for IMO) and bismuth for the sulfur side.

The complication nobody warned me about

Hitting methane hard can unmask a second overgrowth: hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) — the rotten-egg gas from sulfate-reducers like Desulfovibrio. As I cleared the methane, the picture turned into a mixed methane + H₂S overgrowth, which needs a different tool (see the NAC trap in What's dangerous). If your stool suddenly smells like sulfur, you didn't fail — the battlefield shifted.

My own theory: the "sigmoid trap"

Something I came to believe from the inside: the sharp bend between the descending colon and the sigmoid is a natural bottleneck, and I think a great many people have a low-grade version of this — a little methane "paralysis" right at that angle, giving post-carb bloat and unexplained fatigue, never severe enough to get diagnosed. In people like me it climbed the whole tract into full IMO. If you're at the mild end, the same levers — fasting, motility, lower fermentation — may be your whole fix, long before antibiotics.

The hidden cause of back pain & sciatica

The worst pain of all wasn't even in my gut — it was my lower back and sciatica, pressed on by the trapped gas balloon from the inside. A distended, paralyzed bowel pushes directly against the lumbar spine and the sciatic nerve. So many people get back surgery, or live in chronic pain, never knowing a trapped gas pocket is the real driver. If your back or sciatic pain rises and falls with your bloating and bowel movements, suspect the gut before the disc.

The whole protocol in one frame

The 5 pillars: Starve · Disrupt · Kill · Move · Support

Everything I did fits five jobs. Miss one and the other four underperform. The single biggest lesson of eight years: diet is ~90% of the cure. The antimicrobials are the final 10% surgical strike on a starved, weakened enemy.

Pillar 1 · Starve

Carnivore + bone broth

Zero fermentable carbs and fiber = zero fuel for the archaea and their hydrogen-producing partners. Pressure-cooked bone broth doubles as gut-lining medicine (glycine, proline, collagen, glutamine). Foundation, not a side dish.

Pillar 2 · Disrupt

Break the biofilm — daily

A biofilm disruptor on an empty stomach every morning, so the kill agents reach the bugs. Sporadic = useless. My disruptor changed mid-journey (see the NAC trap); berberine also disrupts biofilm while it kills.

Pillar 3 · Kill

Two-front antimicrobials

Upper GI: cinnamon + black seed in water. Lower GI: berberine + allicin (+ Rx rifaximin / metronidazole), with a statin "membrane-softening" layer against the archaea. Standard doses against a starved enemy — not megadoses against a fed one.

Pillar 4 · Move

Restore the MMC

A prokinetic at night on an empty stomach + long fasting windows + ginger + physical techniques + mechanical clearance. The pillar that stops relapse. Prucalopride (Resolor) is the prescription option — but ginger is a genuine natural prokinetic and can absolutely be used instead.

Pillar 5 · Support

Survive & rebuild

Binders, electrolytes (real ones), glycine, gut-lining repair (bone broth, peptides), bile/enzyme support, sleep. Killing a big microbial load floods you with endotoxin; this is how you stay functional while it happens.

The synergy

Starve → Disrupt → Move → Kill

Starve them out of the biofilm desperate and weak. Break the shield at dawn. Let the MMC sweep them into the kill zone overnight. Hit the exposed, active bugs. Repeat until the gut goes quiet.

A walkthrough of what to actually expect

The timeline: Day 0 to one year

Healing is not linear. It comes in waves, with loud days and quiet days. Knowing the map ahead of time is half the battle — most people quit during a die-off wave because they think they're getting worse. Here's the terrain, phase by phase, including the strange signs that are actually good.

The carnivore arc, honestly

Weeks 1–2: hell — the keto adaptation is brutal (this is the electrolyte stretch; push through it, don't reach for coffee). Weeks 3–4: a gift — two of the best weeks of energy and plain enjoyment of life I'd had in years. Then the real work begins: the overgrowth dies and clears section by section down the GI, each pack of debris riding down to the sigmoid and backing up toward the transverse colon. Don't mistake the week-3 honeymoon for the finish line — the deep clearing comes after it, and it comes in waves.

Phase 0Before Day 1

Prepare & confirm

Get a real diagnosis — ideally a breath test measuring all three gases (H₂, CH₄, H₂S), not just hydrogen. Line up a doctor willing to supervise. Stock the kitchen (meat, salt, bones for broth), buy real electrolytes, and pick your start date. Tell your doctor the full plan so nothing surprises them. If your gut lining is wrecked from years of this, this is also where terrain-repair tools (peptides like BPC-157/TB-500, bone broth) start earning their keep. And fix the frame first: if sitting causes tailbone numbness or your pain shifts with position, get your spine and pelvic floor assessed — a chiropractor realigned my spine and ended my tailbone numbness before any gut work began (see Mechanical clearance).

Phase 1Days 1–15

Adaptation + the upper-GI clear

Carnivore begins and the keto/carnivore "flu" hits — crushing fatigue, weakness, headaches — driven mostly by electrolyte loss as you dump stored water. Fixable in hours with electrolytes; do not reach for coffee. At the same time you start the upper-GI antimicrobials (cinnamon + black seed in water). Expect the "rats under the ribcage" and a burp-storm — that's the upper-GI bacteria dying and the MMC firing. The smell here is often sulfur/gas. Die-off (Herxheimer) is real: more bloat, fog, flu-aches.

What it feels like: "I feel worse — is this working?" Yes. Hardest stretch. Binders, electrolytes, broth, sleep.

Phase 2Days 15–30

Burping stops · biofilm breaks · lower-GI begins

When the burping stops completely, the upper GI is largely clear — now the war moves down. Add the lower-GI agents (berberine, then allicin) against the archaea. Biofilm disruption reaches deeper layers and you may see the spider-web material. Motility wakes up — the first time you pass gas freely after months of paralyzed bloat is a genuine milestone. Smells shift from sulfur toward deeper, older putrefaction as trapped debris moves.

What it feels like: real relief between flares. The trapped-bloat cycle is breaking.

Phase 3Days 30–90

Deep clearance — the long, strange middle

The marathon. The body clears years of trapped debris in waves. Expect a "rotten beef"/putrefaction smell (old protein leaving, not new infection), loud bubbling, a stubborn sigmoid "pouch" of gas that often needs mechanical help, intermittent sulfur every few days, and even new night sweats (your autonomic system and metabolism switching back on). Improvement is gradual, not dramatic. This is also when "stuck, dry, undigested" debris shows up — the fix is bile/fat/hydration (TUDCA, ox bile), not force.

What it feels like: "why is there still so much?" Because there was that much in there. Each wave is finite. This is the minimum 90-day window — do not stop here even if you feel good.

Phase 4Days 90–180

Rebuild & careful reintroduction

The gut goes quiet for longer and longer. Once the sulfur smell has been silent for ~2 weeks, you begin cautious reintroduction — one low-risk variable at a time, 48-hour observation each (I started with egg yolks before whites). Keep the prokinetic running. If you need to eat big to work and train, this is where you rebuild digestive capacity (betaine HCl, ox bile/TUDCA, enzymes, ginger before meals) so large meals stop punishing you — the goal is to eat more, not restrict forever.

What it feels like: cautious optimism. You're testing the edges of your new normal.

Phase 56–12 months

Maintenance & relapse-proofing

The MMC is the long game. Keep a prokinetic and a lower-fermentation pattern going 3–6 months minimum to prevent relapse, and find your personal carb threshold one food at a time — the archaea are suppressed, not gone; they wake if you feed their hydrogen. Re-test with a breath test to confirm clearance — don't rely on feeling alone. By 12 months, a body that was paralyzed should feel like a body again.

What it feels like: freedom — earned, and protected.

The smell map (your free diagnostic)

Sulfur / rotten eggs → active H₂S bacteria dying.  •  Rotten beef / putrefaction → old trapped protein debris clearing (good news).  •  Smell fading to nothing for 2+ weeks → the signal to start reintroductions.

Electrolytes ≠ just salt — and skip the gum-filled powders

Pink/Himalayan salt is mostly sodium + chloride with only trace potassium and magnesium. With high water intake, night sweats and a big meat load I was short on potassium and magnesium — the two that prevent cramps, palpitations, poor sleep and fatigue. Add them deliberately: potassium (citrate or bicarbonate; citrate also helps buffer carnivore acidity) and magnesium glycinate at night (not citrate — it's a laxative that can worsen loose, sulfur stool). And avoid commercial electrolyte powders full of guar/acacia/xanthan gums and "natural flavor" — those are fermentable FODMAPs that feed the very bugs you're starving.

The clean morning version I settled on: Kalahari salt (desert rock salt — no sea microplastics) + magnesium glycinate + potassium ascorbate (gentle, buffered), blended into the first water and sipped slowly — gulping minerals on an empty stomach is what triggers the bloat, not the minerals themselves.

A note on "primers" — ivermectin & peptides

A year before I cracked it I'd taken ivermectin (anti-parasitic) and run a 3-month peptide course. Neither cured the IMO — ivermectin isn't a strong enough antimicrobial alone — but both plausibly prepared the terrain: lowering gut inflammation, nudging the microbiome, mildly destabilizing biofilm, and repairing the lining so that when I finally starved and struck the overgrowth, the ground could recover. Worth knowing if you're wondering whether past treatments "did nothing." They may have set the table.

The part most guides won't tell you

Mechanical & structural clearance: releasing the angle

When methane and H₂S switch the colon muscle off, peristalsis stops and gas and stool lock at the sigmoid and the sharp rectosigmoid angle. Chemistry alone can't move what's already paralyzed and stuck. For long stretches, mechanical and structural clearance was what kept me alive and functioning — and it is medically coherent, not desperation. The pelvic floor, the tailbone, the spine and that angle are one mechanical system.

Start structural: the spine and the tailbone

Before antibiotics — before any of the gut protocol — I had to fix the frame the gut hangs on. A chiropractor restored my spine alignment, and that alone shut off the numbness in my tailbone every time I sat, the legacy of a crooked-healed coccyx that had made sitting torture for years. If the structure is jammed, the gut can't drain no matter how well you eat. And be careful with sitting itself: for me, prolonged sitting pressed on the angle and re-paralyzed it, costing days to recover — get up, lie down, change position often. If sitting causes tailbone numbness or your pain shifts with posture, see a chiropractor and/or a pelvic-floor physical therapist first.

Glycerin suppositories — the unlock

A glycerin suppository triggers the local defecation reflex in the rectum, bypassing the methane-induced paralysis entirely. It relaxes the puborectalis and briefly straightens the anorectal/rectosigmoid angle, so a trapped pocket can finally release. On bad days I used them in volume — typically 2–10 a day, sometimes 15, and on the worst days more than 20, each for ~20 seconds. Each one emptied one colonic pocket (haustrum); the next pocket would slide down and refill, so it felt like recurrence but was actually sequential clearance of a years-long backlog. The fact that each one worked proved there was no true blockage — just an enormous volume of debris and a muscle that couldn't move it.

The inversion technique — your own physical prokinetic

I used a back-extension/"spine-extender" frame upside-down so it pressed into my very low belly. Folding into it over the right lower quadrant (the ileocecal valve and ascending colon) produced an immediate urge to go — I was manually performing a sigmoid-angle release and colonic massage, acting as my own physical prokinetic. Best fasted, late afternoon, gentle, with deep belly breathing. Knees-to-chest, child's pose and walking do a softer version of the same thing.

Breathing to release the angle (4 in, 4 out) — crucial

This one was non-negotiable for me. Lie on your side and breathe only into the lower belly, pushing the area under the navel out as far as it will go to release the tightness at the angle and coax the debris along. Count 4 seconds in, 4 seconds out; on each out-breath consciously let the muscles around the rectosigmoid angle go completely slack for those 4 seconds, then breathe in again. Slow, deliberate, repeated. It directly un-paralyzes the angle and moves a trapped pocket without any force.

The levator ani — release it and train it

The levator ani — the sling of pelvic-floor muscle around the rectum — is the gatekeeper of the exit. In a braced, fearful body it does two opposite wrong things: it clenches when it should let go (so nothing passes and sitting hurts), and over years it grows weak and uncoordinated (so it can't generate a clean, complete evacuation). The fix is both directions:

  • Release / down-train (most important first). A levator-ani-focused yoga set — deep supported squat, happy-baby, child's pose, legs-up-the-wall, knees-to-chest — plus the "let it fully drop" on every out-breath. This takes the clamp off so you stop fighting your own floor.
  • Then strengthen & coordinate. Once it can relax on command, train it like any muscle — a small "pelvic-floor mini-gym": slow controlled lifts and full releases, the bulge-on-exhale evacuation pattern, and breath-coordinated reps, so it can do a strong, no-strain push when you actually need one.

Release before strength, always — strengthening a floor that can't relax just tightens the gate. A pelvic-floor physical therapist is the right person to program both halves for your body.

Impact & movement — shake the debris loose

A stalled gut also responds to mechanical force. Brisk walking, and at the right stage controlled impact — light jumping, footfall, uneven terrain — sends rhythmic oscillation through the trunk that physically jostles trapped gas and stool and stimulates peristalsis (the well-known "runner's gut"). Jumping may slam stuck debris loose faster than gentle movement alone. Build it gradually, never during an acute die-off or with an unscreened spine/pelvic-floor — and pair it with the walks after every meal.

Cold mineral-water immersion — for the burning & pressure

In my highest-inflammation periods (2+ years ago), there was a burning under my navel and a bowel pressure that nothing touched. The only thing that calmed it was lowering my lower body into cold mineral-spring water two or three times a day — at 2 a.m., in the rain, in the cold, never missing a day. It's a coping tool, not a cure (likely vagal, anti-inflammatory, vasoconstrictive relief), but when you're in that fire it's real, and I won't leave it out.

Use mechanical tools honestly

These are real tools — but the goal is to make them unnecessary as the MMC comes back online. Chronic suppository use can irritate the rectum and blunt the natural reflex, so always try positioning, breathing and walking first, and treat heavy days as a sign to attack the cause (motility + the "not-ready debris" fix below), not just the symptom.

Why the debris was "stuck" — and the real fix

If what reaches the sigmoid is undigested, dry or sticky, a suppository forces it but it doesn't pass cleanly. The deeper fix is making debris "ready": hydrate with electrolytes through the day, add fat for lubrication and bile flow, use TUDCA / ox bile to thin bile and finish fat digestion, betaine HCl + enzymes so less undigested protein/fat arrives, and magnesium to draw water in. Fewer suppositories follow from better digestion upstream — not from forcing harder downstream.

The 90% that does the work

The diet, precisely

The plate: fresh, cooked meat · fish · eggs (later) · animal fats · pressure-cooked bone broth · salt · water. That's it during the active phase — a zero-residue, zero-fermentation gut so the archaea starve while the antimicrobials finish them. In the strictest stretch I went beef-only (no butter, organs or eggs) specifically to cut the sulfur amino acids feeding the H₂S side.

Hard-won specifics

  • Bone broth is medicine. Pressure-cook bones 3+ hours; 1–2 cups daily, sipped warm, as your electrolyte + repair base.
  • Eggs are green-light — but later. They don't feed archaea (no fermentable carbs), but egg whites are high in sulfur amino acids; if you still have H₂S, reintroduce yolks first, whites much later, and only after the sulfur smell is gone for weeks.
  • Cook your meat. Even kosher heavy-salting only cuts surface pathogens ~85–96% — it doesn't sterilize. During a fragile clearance phase, raw meat is an unnecessary gamble. Sear or roast.
  • Completely fresh beats frozen. The longer meat/fish ages, freezes-and-thaws, or sits as leftovers, the more its protein breaks down into histamine and into sulfur compounds that feed H₂S. Fresh, promptly-cooked carries the least of both — buy fresh, cook fresh, don't hoard leftovers.
  • Cook in MCT oil, then coconut oil — not heavy beef fat. Lean protein with no fat is hard on the gut; heavy animal fat needs bile you may lack and can stall the lower gut. MCT oil is absorbed in the upper GI (no bile), giving the protein its fat without a downstream load; step toward coconut oil as you heal.
  • My lettuce exception. Romaine/iceberg (insoluble cellulose, very low FODMAP) reliably helped my motility without feeding the bugs — a "broom," not a food. But ~95% of IMO patients are made worse by greens. Test with a clean challenge; drop it the instant you get upper-GI bloat.
  • Reintroduce by threshold. The archaea are suppressed, not gone. Test one food at a time (e.g. a little freshly cooked warm white rice — never cooled/resistant-starch) and learn your personal carb tolerance.
  • Irritants out: black pepper, chili/capsaicin, nightshades, bell peppers — all irritants in a raw gut. I had to stop black pepper and extend my timeline because of it.
How to deal with each issue

The symptom playbook

The specific problems that will come up, and how I handled each. Bookmark this one for a bad day.

Crushing fatigue (Days 1–14)

Carnivore / keto flu

Electrolyte loss, not failure. ~1 L water + ½ tsp salt + ¼ tsp potassium + magnesium, sipped over 2 hours. Fatty meat for fuel, salt everything, rest. Relief in 1–2 hours. No coffee.

Worse before better

Die-off (Herxheimer)

Bloat, fog, aches as bugs die. Binders (2+ hrs from everything), aggressive electrolytes + broth, sleep. "Start low, go slow" on kill agents.

Trapped, paralyzed bloat

The stubborn "pouch"

Methane/H₂S switch the colon muscle off — pushing is futile and strains the pelvic floor. Work with it: suppository to open the angle, inversion, knees-to-chest, walking, breathing. Hydrate to keep stool soft.

Back & nerve pain

Sciatica from trapped gas

If your back or sciatic pain rises and falls with bloating, suspect the gas balloon pressing your spine — not a disc. Clear the gas (4-4 breathing, angle release, motility) and the "back" pain often goes with it. Many avoid needless surgery this way.

The reset that always worked

4-in, 4-out breathing

Lie on your side. Breathe only into the lower belly, pushing the area under the navel out to release the angle. 4 sec in, 4 sec out, letting the pelvic-floor muscles fully slacken on the out-breath. Slow and repeated — it un-paralyzes the angle without force.

Different gas, different feel

"Easy" vs "stubborn" gas

H₂/CO₂ pass easily; methane & H₂S "paralyze" the muscle and feel stuck. Easy gas = a cleared pocket. Stubborn gas = a holdout — reach for the mechanical tools.

Rotten-egg stool

Sulfur smell appears

H₂S surfacing. Lower sulfur input (ease off eggs/whites & high-dose NAC), molybdenum + Epsom-salt baths, ask about bismuth + a triple-gas breath test.

The coffee craving

Coffee & decaf

Coffee forces a chaotic colon dump and depletes the clean MMC sweep — decaf is nearly as irritating. If at all, mid-afternoon with food, far from the prokinetic window. Better: ginger tea, broth, dandelion-root or rooibos.

Burning under the navel

Relentless lower-belly fire

What worked when nothing else did: cold mineral-water immersion of the lower body, 2–3×/day. Plus lying down, broth, electrolytes.

Can't eat enough to train/work

Big meals without bloat

Don't eat less — digest more. Betaine HCl + ox bile/TUDCA + enzymes with big meals, ginger 15–20 min before, sip (don't chug), short walk after. Wean off as digestion returns.

New night sweats

Your engine restarting

Never sweated, now do at night? Usually a good sign — autonomic reset, returning metabolism, immune activation. Replace salt/water, breathable bedding; worry only with true fever (>38°C).

Bloat that's actually your bladder

Referred pelvic pain

A full bladder, compressed when you sit, mimics lower-GI pain. Pee proactively before sitting. (For me this overlapped a crooked-healed tailbone — pelvic-floor issues can masquerade as "gut" pain; a pelvic-floor PT is worth it.)

"It empties then refills"

Sequential pocket clearance

Relief then re-bloat = emptying the colon's pouches one at a time; the next slides in. A finite backlog, not an endless problem. Support the movement; don't force it.

Dietary slip happened

Damage control

One slip isn't relapse if you respond right: cinnamon to blunt fermentation, DGL to soothe, back to strict carnivore + broth 24–48 h, binder, don't skip the prokinetic that night.

Red flags — stop and get medical care

Numbness/tingling in hands or feet or seizures (possible metronidazole neurotoxicity) · uncontrollable vomiting or inability to keep fluids down · severe or new abdominal pain · rash/swelling/trouble breathing · watery diarrhea >3×/day with fever (possible C. diff) · yellowing skin/eyes, dark urine, or severe muscle pain (liver/statin) · confusion or tremors (bismuth overuse) · fast weight loss or blood in stool. None of these are "push through it" symptoms.

Genuinely dangerous — do not

These can hurt you
  • Alcohol of any kind (incl. "digestive" tequila). With a benzodiazepine (Librax) it's a potentially lethal CNS-depressant combo; with metronidazole it triggers a violent disulfiram reaction. It also wrecks the gut lining and liver mid-treatment.
  • Aloe ferox / aggressive herbal laxatives. Drastic cathartics — cramping, electrolyte depletion, contraindicated in inflamed/obstructed bowels. (OTC aloe laxatives were pulled by the FDA over safety.)
  • Capsaicin capsules. A mucosal irritant and histamine liberator — it forces motility by irritation, traumatizing an already raw gut.
  • Water enemas / "colon cleanses." Risk of perforation in a distended, fragile sigmoid, electrolyte imbalance, and flushing out the good flora you're regrowing. No proven benefit. (Targeted glycerin suppositories are a far more localized tool — see Mechanical clearance.)
  • Terbinafine for "fungus." A liver-toxic antifungal with no activity against bacteria or archaea — wrong target, real risk.
  • Bismuth overuse. Effective short-term for H₂S, but don't exceed label limits or run it long-term — bismuth neurotoxicity (confusion, tremor) is real.
  • Stopping a doctor's plan on a hunch. I once nearly dropped metronidazole and the statin "surface-flattening," which would have removed two of the most strategic weapons. Talk to the prescriber; don't unilaterally pull a load-bearing piece.
On suppositories — a correction to the usual advice

Standard advice treats heavy suppository use as pure dependence. In a methane-paralyzed colon it is also a legitimate mechanical technique for opening the rectosigmoid angle and clearing pockets the muscle can't move (see Mechanical clearance). The honest line is balance: use them to survive and to clear the backlog, and drive toward not needing them by restoring motility and digestion. Both things are true.

Straight answers

FAQ

Is 90 days really the minimum?

For me, yes — 90 days on carnivore was the floor, and I extended past it because of a black-pepper slip. Deep clearance (Phase 3) runs roughly Days 30–90, and you can feel "good" mid-phase while there's still a backlog. Full healing landed in the 180-day-to-one-year range, with months of maintenance after. Stopping early is the classic relapse trigger. Confirm with testing, not just feeling.

Were the suppositories a crutch or a treatment?

Both, honestly. In a methane-paralyzed colon, a glycerin suppository mechanically opens the rectosigmoid angle and triggers a reflex the muscle can't, clearing trapped pockets one at a time — that's real and it kept me functioning when I used 10–15+ in a day. The goal is to make them unnecessary by restoring motility (prucalopride, fasting, the MMC) and fixing "not-ready" debris upstream (bile/enzymes/hydration). Use them to survive; aim to outgrow them.

Can I do this without antibiotics?

Maybe, for milder cases — strict diet + biofilm disruption + the berberine/allicin/cinnamon/nigella herbal stack + prokinetics + the statin "surface-flatten" is a real non-antibiotic path, and lower-grade overgrowth can respond to it. For severe IMO the odds drop a lot without the pharmaceutical kill, and die-off is longer. This is the exact conversation to have with a doctor — not a website decision.

What's the difference between the upper-GI and lower-GI tools?

Upper GI (burping, hydrogen) cleared first with cinnamon + black seed blended in water — liquid coats the whole tract. Lower GI (methane archaea + H₂S) is the second front, hit with berberine + allicin and bismuth. Carnivore starves the hydrogen that feeds the archaea across both fronts.

What's the single most important thing?

Motility. The MMC. Everything else buys time; restoring the overnight sweep (prokinetic + long fasting window + movement) is what makes it stay gone. If you fix one thing, fix the sweeper.

How do I know I'm actually healed?

Quiet gut for long stretches · no sulfur smell for 2+ weeks · easy, complete bowel movements without aids · foods reintroduced without flares · and a follow-up breath test confirming clearance. Don't declare victory on feeling alone — re-test.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's one person's documented experience, done under a doctor's care. The prescription pieces are illegal and unsafe to self-source, and a few dead-ends here are dangerous. Bring this to a clinician who can test you, prescribe appropriately, and watch your liver/kidney/electrolytes. Use it to ask sharper questions.
